§ 01 About

A biomedical engineer in the operating room.

I'm a postdoctoral researcher in Biomedical Engineering at Vanderbilt University, working with Dr. Michael I. Miga in the Vanderbilt Institute for Surgery and Engineering (VISE). My work lives at the intersection of image-guided surgery, augmented reality, and 3D computer vision.

For the past five years I've been building a comprehensive mixed-reality surgical navigation system for liver interventions — one that tracks tools in real time, corrects for soft-tissue deformation, and projects the patient's anatomy back onto their body through a head-mounted display. I care about the part where the research leaves the lab: validating systems rigorously, collaborating with clinicians, and pushing technology toward the operating room.

Before Vanderbilt, I studied biomedical engineering at the University of Virginia and Virginia Tech. Along the way I've written MATLAB for dose-volume calculators, turned electron microscopy into 3D colormaps of neuronal activity, and most recently explored how biomedical tools might work in space.

    Selected projects

    Building systems that bridge medical imaging, mixed reality, and the operating room.

    Doctoral thesis · 2020–2025

    Mixed-Reality Surgical Navigation for Liver Surgery

    A comprehensive MR system integrating real-time tool tracking, deformation-correction algorithms, and dynamic 3D anatomical visualization — validated with both surface and subsurface targets for clinical accuracy.

    HoloLens 2 Unity · C# Patent pending
    System · HoloLens 2

    AR In-Situ Ultrasound Visualization

    Live ultrasound imaging rendered in-place on the patient through HoloLens 2, using retroreflective markers for inside-out probe tracking — no external cameras required.

    Inside-out tracking Real-time US stream
    Display system

    Holographic 2D Panel Display for Liver Guidance

    A head-mounted reinterpretation of the four-screen layout surgeons already know from traditional navigation — axial, sagittal, coronal, and 3D — now floating in the room where they need it.

    Robotic Stereo-Camera + SAM 2

    A robotic stereo-camera rig coupled with Segment Anything 2 to reconstruct anatomical surfaces from multiple viewpoints — a fast, non-contact alternative to tracked-stylus digitization.
