§ 01  ·  Research

On seeing inside the body.

My research develops mixed-reality surgical navigation — augmented-reality systems that overlay patient-specific anatomy directly onto the surgical field. Working at the intersection of 3D computer vision, biomechanics, and head-mounted display hardware, I design end-to-end systems that track instruments, compensate for organ deformation, and present stable holographic guidance to the surgeon in real time.

The central question I pursue: how do we make the digital anatomical model trustworthy enough to act on in the operating room? That means quantifying hologram stability, depth sensing accuracy, eye tracking precision, and subsurface target localization — and engineering the pipeline so every number earns the clinic's trust.

i.

Mixed-Reality Surgical Navigation

Self-contained AR guidance systems on HoloLens 2 and Magic Leap 2 for laparoscopic and open liver surgery.

ii.

Intraoperative Surface Acquisition

Comparing contact-based digitization and noncontact depth sensing for model-to-patient registration of soft tissue.

iii.

Deformation Correction

Physics-informed registration pipelines that align preoperative imaging with the intraoperative organ.

iv.

Headset Validation

Quantitative benchmarks for hologram drift, eye-tracking angular error, marker tracking, and depth-camera precision.
